# Leveraging User-Generated Content — Including the Parts That Carry Risk

User-generated content is more persuasive than anything you publish about yourself, costs less to produce, and is the single most common place marketing teams accidentally commit copyright infringement.

Both halves of that are worth taking seriously.

### Key takeaways

- UGC works because it is not from you. Over-polishing it removes the reason it works.
- A public post is not permission to use it. You need explicit rights, especially for paid media.
- Incentivised reviews must be disclosed, and fake reviews are regulated in India.
- Negative content is not a failure of the programme. How you respond to it is the programme.

## Why it works

Prospects discount what a brand says about itself and weight what customers say heavily. That asymmetry is the whole mechanism.

It is also the reason the most common mistake — editing UGC until it sounds like brand copy — destroys the value. A slightly rough photo from a real customer outperforms a polished studio image precisely because it reads as real.

## Where it comes from

**Reviews and ratings.** The highest-intent form. People actively search for them before buying.

**Social posts and tags.** Customers using your product, unprompted.

**Testimonials.** Solicited, more controlled, less credible for exactly that reason.

**Community content.** Forum answers, group discussions, questions answered by other customers.

**Photos and video.** The most valuable for advertising, and the most legally fraught.

## The rights problem

This is the section most guides omit, and it is the one that costs money when ignored.

**A public post is not a licence.** Someone posting a photo tagged with your brand retains copyright. Reposting, resizing, putting it on your website, or — most seriously — running it as a paid ad without permission is infringement, regardless of the tag.

Platform terms of service grant the platform a licence, not you.

**What you need:**

- **Explicit written permission** naming the specific use. A comment reply asking "can we share this?" and a clear yes creates a record. A vague hashtag entry does not.
- **Rights covering paid media separately.** Permission to repost on your feed is not permission to run it as an advertisement. This distinction is routinely missed and is where the real exposure sits.
- **A stated duration.** Permission is not necessarily permanent.
- **Clarity on people in the image.** Copyright belongs to the photographer; the people depicted may have separate rights, particularly if the use is commercial.

For contests and campaigns, put the rights grant in the terms of entry, and make the terms visible rather than buried.

The cost of getting this wrong is a takedown demand, a damages claim, or a public complaint from a customer who feels exploited — the last being the most damaging, because it is exactly the audience the programme depends on.

## Reviews: incentives and the law

**Disclose incentives.** If you gave a discount, a free product or an entry into a draw in exchange for a review, that must be disclosed. In India this falls under the CCPA's endorsement guidelines and ASCI's rules; comparable regimes apply in most markets you would sell into.

**Never write or commission fake reviews.** Beyond being dishonest, it is specifically regulated. India introduced a national standard for online consumer reviews (IS 19000:2022) covering how reviews are collected, moderated and published, and the consumer protection framework treats fabricated reviews as unfair trade practice. Enforcement is uneven; the reputational risk when it surfaces is not.

**Do not filter for positives only.** Suppressing negative reviews is both detectable and counterproductive — a page of uniformly perfect reviews reads as fake, and buyers actively look for the critical ones to judge whether the flaws matter to them.

## Generating UGC

Most UGC does not appear on its own. It has to be prompted.

**Ask at the right moment.** Just after a good experience — delivery, completion, a successful outcome — not weeks later.

**Make it easy.** One link, one field, minimal friction. Every extra step loses most of the remaining people.

**Give a frame.** "Share a photo of your setup" produces more than "share your experience". Specific prompts get specific responses.

**Use a hashtag that is actually distinctive.** Generic ones fill with unrelated content and become useless for discovery.

**Respond to everything.** People who receive a reply post again. People who are ignored do not.

**Feature contributors properly.** Credit by name or handle. Recognition is the main compensation in most UGC programmes and it works — but only if it is visible.

**Incentivise carefully.** Incentives raise volume and lower credibility, and they trigger disclosure obligations. Incentivise participation, never a positive opinion.

## Using it well

**Website** — reviews on product pages, customer photos in context, community answers where people look for them.

**Social** — repost with credit. This is also the cheapest way to encourage more, because contributors see that contributing gets noticed.

**Paid advertising** — UGC-style creative frequently outperforms produced creative on Meta and similar platforms, because it looks like the surrounding feed rather than an interruption. **Confirm your rights cover this use specifically.**

**Email** — reviews and customer photos in lifecycle emails, particularly at the consideration stage.

**Sales material** — real customer language, quoted accurately, is more persuasive than any claim you write yourself.

## Handling negative content

Once you invite contribution, you will receive criticism. This is not a programme failure; it is the programme working.

**Respond publicly, specifically, and without defensiveness.** A well-handled complaint is more persuasive to onlookers than a positive review, because it demonstrates something a testimonial cannot.

**Fix what is fixable and say so.** "You were right, we changed it" is the strongest thing you can publish.

**Do not delete criticism** unless it breaches genuine policy — abuse, spam, or falsehood. Deleting legitimate criticism produces a second, larger complaint about the deletion.

**Moderate for policy, not for sentiment.** Publish the rules and apply them consistently.

## Measuring it

- **Volume and rate of contribution** — is the programme actually generating anything?
- **Conversion difference** on pages with UGC against pages without. This is the clearest evidence of value and is straightforward to test.
- **Performance of UGC creative** against produced creative in paid campaigns.
- **Sentiment over time**, which is a useful early warning on product or service problems.

The conversion comparison is the number worth building first — it is the one that justifies the programme internally.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using content without permission.** The expensive one.
2. **Assuming social permission covers paid ads.** It does not.
3. **Over-editing.** Removes the authenticity that made it work.
4. **Hiding negative reviews.** Detectable and self-defeating.
5. **Failing to disclose incentives.** Regulatory exposure in most markets.
6. **Never responding.** Contribution stops when nobody notices it.

## Conclusion

UGC is the most credible content you have access to and the cheapest to obtain. Ask for it at the right moment, secure rights properly and in writing, disclose anything you incentivised, publish criticism alongside praise, and resist the urge to polish it into something that sounds like you.

The programmes that fail do so for one of two reasons: nobody asked, or somebody used content they had no right to use.

## FAQ

### Can I repost a customer's photo that tagged my brand?

Not automatically. They hold the copyright. Ask for explicit permission and keep the record — particularly before any paid use.

### Do I need separate permission for advertising?

Yes. Permission to share on your own feed does not extend to running the content as a paid advertisement. Ask specifically.

### Can I offer a discount for a review?

You can incentivise leaving a review, but the incentive must be disclosed and it must not be conditional on the review being positive. Fabricated reviews are regulated in India under the consumer protection framework and the national standard for online reviews.

### Should I remove negative reviews?

No, unless they breach a published policy. Respond instead. A well-handled complaint is more persuasive than a testimonial.

### How do I get more UGC?

Ask at the moment of satisfaction, make it a single easy step, give a specific prompt, and visibly credit contributors.

### How do I prove it's working?

Compare conversion rates on pages with UGC against equivalent pages without, and test UGC creative against produced creative in paid campaigns.
