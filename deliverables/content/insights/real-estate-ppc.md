# Real Estate PPC: A Practical Best-Practice Guide

Real estate is an unforgiving category for paid search. The consideration window runs to months, the enquiry-to-booking ratio is brutal, and the cost of a lead is high enough that a badly structured account burns real money before anyone notices.

It is also a category where paid media works, provided the account is built for how people actually buy property rather than how the platform defaults suggest.

### Key takeaways

- Optimise to qualified enquiry, not to raw lead volume. The two diverge sharply in this category.
- Intent varies enormously across property search terms. Structure the account around that variation.
- Landing page and follow-up speed usually cost more deals than bidding does.
- Conversion tracking must survive the handoff to the sales team, or you are optimising against nothing.

## The structural problem

Most underperforming real estate accounts share one flaw: they optimise for lead volume because lead volume is what the platform can see.

A campaign generating a large number of cheap enquiries looks excellent in the ads dashboard and can be worthless. The enquiries that convert to site visits and bookings come from a narrow band of high-intent searches, and those cost several times the blended average. Bidding down to protect the average cost per lead systematically starves the traffic that produces revenue.

The fix is to get qualification data back into the platform. Import qualified-lead status from the CRM so bidding optimises against enquiries the sales team considers real. Until that loop exists, every other optimisation is guesswork.

## Understanding intent in property search

Search terms in this category fall into rough intent tiers, and they should not sit in the same campaign:

**High intent** — a specific project or building name, "price" and "floor plan" modifiers, locality plus configuration ("3 BHK Whitefield"). These convert. They also attract competitor bidding, so they are expensive.

**Mid intent** — locality research, "best area to buy in", builder comparisons. Worth capturing, but expect a longer path and treat them as a nurture entry point rather than a direct conversion source.

**Low intent** — rental queries, market news, career and investment-education searches. These will flood a broad-match campaign and produce enquiries that never convert. Most of the negative keyword work in this category is spent excluding them.

Rent-versus-buy contamination is the single most common leak. If your campaign is selling and your keywords do not explicitly exclude rental terms, you are paying for tenants.

## Building the campaign

### Keywords

Start tighter than feels comfortable. Exact and phrase match on project and locality terms, with broad match introduced only once you have enough conversion data for the algorithm to have something to learn from.

Build the negative keyword list before launch, not after. At minimum: rent, rental, lease, PG, jobs, salary, courses, and the names of any localities you do not serve.

### Geotargeting

Property demand is geographically specific in ways platform defaults do not respect. Set location targeting to *presence* rather than *presence or interest* — otherwise you pay for people who merely searched about your city.

For projects with meaningful out-of-city or NRI demand, run those as separate campaigns with their own budget and messaging. Blending them destroys both.

### Ad copy

State the concrete facts buyers filter on: location, configuration, price band, possession timeline. Vagueness in property advertising reads as evasion, and buyers are practised at spotting it.

Use ad extensions properly — sitelinks to floor plans and locality pages, call extensions with the number your sales team actually answers, and structured snippets for amenities.

### Landing pages

The landing page is where most real estate spend is wasted.

- Match the page to the ad. A generic project page behind a "3 BHK price" ad loses the visitor.
- Lead with the information the search implied — price band, plan, location — before the enquiry form.
- Keep forms short. Name, phone and a single qualifying field. Every additional field costs completions, and in this category the phone number is what matters.
- Test page speed on mobile on a real connection. Most property traffic is mobile, often on patchy networks.

## Measuring and managing

### Track past the click

Set up conversion tracking that records form submissions and calls, then close the loop by importing qualified status from your CRM. Without that, the platform optimises toward whatever is cheap and plentiful.

This is worth stating plainly because it is where accounts fail silently: if platform conversions and CRM records disagree, the platform's bidding is being trained on bad data, and adding budget compounds the error rather than fixing it.

### The metrics that matter

| Metric | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per qualified enquiry | The only cost metric worth managing to |
| Enquiry-to-site-visit rate | Where lead quality actually becomes visible |
| Search term report | The fastest route to finding where money is leaking |
| Impression share on project terms | Whether you are defending your own brand searches |

Cost per lead in isolation is misleading. A lead at three times the price that converts at five times the rate is the better lead.

### Budget

Allocate by campaign performance against qualified enquiries, not by even split. Real estate demand is also seasonal and event-driven — launches, festive periods, regulatory changes — so review allocation monthly rather than setting it once.

## Where PPC fits alongside everything else

Paid search captures existing demand. It does not create it. For a new launch with no established search volume for the project name, search alone will underdeliver, and the account will look broken when the actual problem is that nobody is searching yet.

That is where demand generation — social, video, display — has to run first, so that branded search volume exists for search to capture. Accounts that skip this step and then blame the search campaign are diagnosing the wrong thing.

## Common mistakes

1. **Optimising to cost per lead.** Covered above; it is the big one.
2. **No negative keyword discipline.** Rental and jobs traffic will find you.
3. **One campaign for all projects.** Different price points need different budgets, messaging and landing pages.
4. **Slow follow-up.** Property enquiries decay fast. A lead contacted within minutes converts at a materially different rate than one contacted the next day, and no amount of bid optimisation compensates for a slow sales desk.
5. **Ignoring the search term report.** It is the cheapest optimisation available and the most frequently skipped.

## Conclusion

Structure the account around intent, exclude the traffic that cannot convert, get qualified-lead data back into the platform, and make sure the follow-up is fast enough to justify the media spend.

Most real estate PPC problems are not bidding problems. They are tracking problems, landing page problems, or sales-response problems that show up as bad media performance.

## FAQ

### What is the biggest mistake in real estate PPC?

Optimising to cost per lead. It systematically defunds the high-intent traffic that produces bookings.

### How should I structure campaigns?

By intent tier and by project. Project-name and configuration searches should not share a budget with locality research.

### What negative keywords do I need?

Rent, rental, lease, PG, jobs, salary, courses, and every locality you do not serve. Build the list before launch.

### Which platforms work?

Google Ads for capturing search intent; Meta for demand generation and remarketing; portal advertising as a supplement. In India that means MagicBricks, 99acres and Housing.com; in the UAE, Property Finder and Bayut.

### How do I know if my lead quality is bad?

Compare platform-reported conversions against qualified enquiries in your CRM. A large gap means the account is being optimised against the wrong signal.

### Why isn't my campaign working for a new launch?

Possibly because there is no demand to capture yet. Search works on existing intent; a project nobody has heard of has none. Demand generation has to come first.
