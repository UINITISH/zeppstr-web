# Choosing E-commerce Email Marketing Software

Most e-commerce operators choose an email platform first and think about what to send afterwards. That is the wrong order, and it is why so many end up paying for automation features they never configure.

The platform matters. It matters considerably less than whether you have built the four flows that produce most of the revenue.

### Key takeaways

- A handful of automated flows generate a disproportionate share of e-commerce email revenue. Build those before comparing platforms.
- The real differentiator between platforms is the depth of the e-commerce data integration, not the template editor.
- Pricing scales with list size, so a bloated list costs you twice — in fees and in deliverability.
- Migration is painful. Choose for where you will be in two years.

## Build the flows first

Before evaluating software, know what you intend to automate. In most e-commerce accounts, automated flows outperform broadcast campaigns per recipient by a wide margin, because they arrive when the customer is already doing something relevant.

**The four that matter most:**

**Welcome series.** Triggered on signup. The highest-engagement moment you will ever have with a subscriber, and the one most often left as a single confirmation email.

**Abandoned cart.** Triggered on cart abandonment. Timing is the mechanism here — an hour later performs very differently from the next day. Usually a short sequence rather than one message.

**Browse abandonment.** Triggered on viewing a product without adding to cart. Lower intent than cart abandonment, larger audience, and frequently not built at all.

**Post-purchase.** Order confirmation, shipping updates, then a sequence toward review, replenishment or complementary products. This is where repeat purchase rate is made.

Add win-back for lapsed customers and replenishment for consumables where relevant.

**Now evaluate platforms on how well they support these**, not on how many templates they ship with.

## What actually differentiates platforms

### Depth of e-commerce integration

This is the real decision. Does the platform receive product-level data — what was viewed, what was in the cart, what was bought, at what price, how often — or only an email address and an order total?

Deep integration lets you segment on purchase behaviour and populate emails with the specific products a customer looked at. Shallow integration means your "personalisation" is a first name.

Check what the integration actually syncs before committing, not what the marketing page implies.

### Segmentation model

Can you build a segment like "bought category X twice in six months but nothing in the last ninety days"? That question separates platforms built for e-commerce from platforms built for newsletters.

### Deliverability infrastructure

Shared versus dedicated IP options, authentication support, and whether the provider actively manages sending reputation. This is invisible until it fails, at which point it is the only thing that matters.

### Pricing model

Most price on list size or send volume. Understand which, and model the cost at three times your current list — that is roughly when you will notice.

Note the incentive this creates: platforms charge for subscribers whether or not they engage. Keeping unengaged subscribers costs you money *and* damages deliverability. Cull on a schedule.

## The main options

Rather than a pricing table that will be wrong within months — check current pricing directly, it changes frequently — here is what each is genuinely for.

**Klaviyo** — built specifically for e-commerce. The deepest product and behavioural data integration, the strongest segmentation, and the flow builder most suited to the sequences above. Priced accordingly, and the cost scales quickly with list size. The right choice once email revenue justifies it.

**Mailchimp** — broad, accessible, familiar. Adequate e-commerce features, less depth than Klaviyo on behavioural segmentation. Reasonable for smaller stores or where email is a secondary channel.

**Brevo** (formerly SendinBlue — renamed in 2023, and still listed under the old name in a great deal of published guidance) — good value, includes SMS, prices on sends rather than contacts, which suits large lists sent to infrequently.

**Omnisend** — e-commerce focused, combines email and SMS, positioned between Mailchimp and Klaviyo on both capability and cost.

**MailerLite** — simple and inexpensive. Suitable for small stores that need reliable sending and basic automation without paying for depth they will not use.

**Shopify Email** — if you are on Shopify and sending occasionally, the built-in option may be sufficient. It will not take you far, but it costs almost nothing to start.

**GetResponse** — broader marketing platform including landing pages and webinars. Worth considering if you want those in one place.

## Choosing

Work through these in order:

1. **What is your e-commerce platform?** Integration depth varies by combination. A platform with excellent Shopify support may integrate poorly with WooCommerce.
2. **What is your list size, and its trajectory?** Pricing models diverge sharply at scale.
3. **How sophisticated does your segmentation need to be?** Be honest. Buying advanced segmentation you will not configure is the most common overspend here.
4. **Who will operate it?** A powerful platform nobody has time to learn produces worse results than a simple one used properly.
5. **What does migration cost?** Moving platforms means rebuilding every flow, re-authenticating your domain and warming a new sending reputation. Choose for two years out.

## Getting the setup right

**Authenticate your domain.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Since February 2024, Google and Yahoo require this from bulk senders. Every platform documents it; many operators skip it and then wonder why delivery is poor.

**Verify the integration is syncing.** Place a test order and confirm the data appears where you expect.

**Build the welcome flow before anything else.** Highest return, least effort.

**Set up suppression properly.** Hard bounces removed automatically, unsubscribes honoured across all sends.

**Connect revenue tracking.** You want to attribute revenue to flows and campaigns, or you cannot tell which are worth maintaining.

## Measuring

For e-commerce specifically:

- **Revenue per recipient** — the honest measure, and unaffected by open-rate distortion.
- **Flow revenue against campaign revenue** — flows should be carrying a substantial share.
- **Repeat purchase rate** among email subscribers versus non-subscribers.
- **List health** — bounce rate, unsubscribe rate, and complaint rate against the 0.3% threshold major providers now apply.

Open rate is no longer reliable enough to manage to; privacy protections inflate it unpredictably.

## Conclusion

Decide what you are going to automate, then choose the platform that supports it and integrates properly with your store. Check pricing directly rather than trusting any published table, including this one's predecessors — it changes constantly.

And build the welcome and abandoned cart flows before you spend another week comparing feature lists. They will earn more than the difference between any two platforms on this page.

## FAQ

### What is the best email platform for e-commerce?

Klaviyo, if email revenue justifies the cost and you need deep behavioural segmentation. Mailchimp, Brevo or Omnisend for smaller stores. The decision depends more on your store platform and list size than on any ranking.

### What should I automate first?

Welcome series, then abandoned cart. Those two produce the largest return for the least work.

### How much does it cost?

Pricing scales with list size or send volume and changes frequently. Model it at three times your current list, and check current pricing directly.

### Does the platform choice really matter?

Less than the flows you build. A well-configured basic platform outperforms an advanced one nobody has set up.

### Should I switch platforms?

Only for a real capability gap. Migration means rebuilding every flow and re-warming your sending reputation — weeks of work and a temporary dip in deliverability.

### Why is my delivery poor?

Check authentication first. Since February 2024 Google and Yahoo require SPF, DKIM and DMARC from bulk senders, and failing this looks identical to a list quality problem from the dashboard.
