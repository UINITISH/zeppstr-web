# Amazon PPC: A Working Strategy for Sellers

Amazon advertising has a structural difference from every other paid channel: the ads compete with the organic results, and the organic results are partly determined by sales, which are partly determined by the ads.

That loop is the whole game. Sellers who treat Amazon PPC as ordinary search advertising manage it to the wrong metric and miss the mechanism that actually makes it worth running.

### Key takeaways

- ACoS measures the ad campaign. TACoS measures the business. Manage to TACoS.
- Ad-driven sales improve organic rank, which reduces future ad dependence. That is the point.
- The auto-to-manual harvesting workflow is the core operating practice.
- Your listing converts or it does not. Advertising a poor listing accelerates the loss.

## The ad formats

**Sponsored Products** — the workhorse. Individual product ads in search results and on product detail pages. Available to most sellers, and where the majority of spend and return sits.

**Sponsored Brands** — banner-style ads with your logo, a headline and multiple products. Requires Brand Registry. Good for category-level terms and for defending your own brand searches.

**Sponsored Display** — retargeting on and off Amazon, and placement on competitor detail pages. Useful for defence and for re-reaching people who viewed but did not buy.

Most sellers should build Sponsored Products properly before touching the other two.

## The metric that matters

**ACoS** (Advertising Cost of Sales) is ad spend divided by ad-attributed revenue. It tells you how efficient the campaign is in isolation.

**TACoS** (Total Advertising Cost of Sales) is ad spend divided by *total* revenue, including organic. This is the number that reflects the business.

The distinction matters because of the loop. Ad-driven sales contribute to your sales velocity, which improves organic ranking, which produces organic sales you did not pay for. A campaign with mediocre ACoS may be producing excellent TACoS by lifting organic performance.

**Falling TACoS while revenue grows** is the pattern you want: advertising is building organic position, and each rupee of spend supports more total revenue over time.

**Rising TACoS** means you are becoming more dependent on ads to hold the same revenue — a warning sign even if ACoS looks fine.

There is no universal target ACoS. It depends on your margin. Work out your break-even ACoS from contribution margin, then decide whether you are bidding for profit today or for rank.

## Before you spend anything

**Fix the listing first.** Advertising sends traffic to a page that either converts or does not. If conversion is poor, advertising accelerates the loss and damages your metrics.

Check: main image quality and compliance, title clarity, bullet points that answer real objections, A+ content if you have Brand Registry, review count and rating, and price against the competitors appearing alongside you.

**Confirm you can fulfil.** Advertising a product that goes out of stock wastes spend and disrupts ranking momentum. Pause campaigns before stock runs out, not after.

## The core workflow: auto to manual

This is the operating practice that most distinguishes competent Amazon PPC from amateur.

**1. Run an automatic campaign.** Amazon matches your product to search terms it considers relevant. This is deliberately broad, and it is doing keyword research for you using Amazon's own data on what your product resembles.

**2. Let it gather data.** Two to four weeks, depending on volume. Resist optimising early.

**3. Read the search term report.** It shows the actual queries that produced clicks and, more importantly, sales.

**4. Harvest.** Move converting search terms into a manual campaign as exact match, where you control the bid.

**5. Negate.** Add the terms that spent without converting as negatives in the automatic campaign, so it stops paying for them.

**6. Repeat.** The automatic campaign keeps discovering; the manual campaign holds what works.

This cycle, run consistently, does more for account performance than any bidding strategy.

## Campaign structure

**Separate by intent and control.**

- **Automatic campaigns** for discovery, with a modest budget.
- **Manual exact** for proven converting terms, at controlled bids.
- **Manual phrase or broad** for expansion around proven terms.
- **Brand defence** on your own brand name — cheap, high-converting, and protects against competitors bidding on you.
- **Competitor targeting** via product targeting on rival ASINs, if margins allow.

**Separate high-value products into their own campaigns.** Sharing a budget between your best seller and a slow mover means the slow mover consumes budget the best seller could have used.

## Bidding

Start moderate rather than aggressive. Amazon's bid recommendations skew high — they are recommendations from a party that benefits from higher bids.

**Placement adjustments** are one of the more useful controls. Top-of-search placement typically converts better and costs more; if your data supports it, a placement multiplier there is often more efficient than raising your base bid across all placements.

**Adjust on data, not on impatience.** Give changes at least a week before judging. Amazon's attribution window means conversions arrive after the click, sometimes days later, and reacting to incomplete data is how accounts get destabilised.

## Reading the reports

The **search term report** is the most valuable document in the account. Weekly:

- Which terms produced sales, and at what ACoS?
- Which spent without converting? Negate them.
- Which converted well at low volume? Increase the bid.
- Are irrelevant terms appearing? Your match types are too loose.

Also watch **impression share and top-of-search share** on your priority terms, and **conversion rate by term** — a term with high clicks and no conversions usually means the query does not match what you are actually selling.

## Common mistakes

1. **Managing to ACoS alone.** Misses the organic contribution entirely.
2. **Advertising a poorly-converting listing.** Fix the page first.
3. **Never negating.** The single largest source of waste.
4. **One campaign for everything.** Removes all control over budget allocation.
5. **Reacting too fast.** Attribution lags; changes need time to produce data.
6. **Ignoring stock.** Running out mid-campaign wastes spend and costs ranking momentum.
7. **Accepting Amazon's suggested bids.** Treat them as an upper bound.

## Where this is heading

Amazon's ad platform continues to expand — video placements in search results, broader off-Amazon display inventory, and increasingly automated bidding.

Automated bidding is worth testing once you have solid conversion history, on the same principle as elsewhere: the algorithms need data to be useful, and applying them to a new account with no conversion signal produces expensive noise.

For sellers on Amazon India specifically, competition varies enormously by category, and several categories remain considerably less contested than their equivalents in mature markets. That gap tends to close; the useful move is to establish organic rank in a category while advertising is still cheap enough to fund it.

## Conclusion

Fix the listing, run automatic campaigns to discover terms, harvest what converts into manual campaigns, negate what does not, and judge the whole thing on TACoS rather than ACoS.

The reason to run Amazon PPC is not the ad-attributed sales. It is that ad-driven velocity builds organic rank, and organic rank is the asset. Manage it accordingly.

## FAQ

### What is a good ACoS?

It depends on your margin. Calculate your break-even ACoS from contribution margin, then decide whether you are optimising for profit now or buying rank. There is no universal figure.

### What is the difference between ACoS and TACoS?

ACoS uses ad-attributed revenue only. TACoS uses total revenue including organic. TACoS reflects whether advertising is building the business or merely sustaining it.

### Should I start with automatic or manual campaigns?

Automatic. Let Amazon show you which search terms convert, then harvest those into manual campaigns where you control bids.

### How often should I optimise?

Weekly for search term review and negatives. Give bid changes at least a week before judging, since attribution lags the click.

### Why is my ACoS high?

Usually a listing that does not convert, terms that do not match what you sell, or bids set from Amazon's recommendations. Check conversion rate by search term first.

### Do ads help organic ranking?

Indirectly and substantially. Ad-driven sales contribute to sales velocity, which is a ranking input. This is why TACoS is the better management metric.
