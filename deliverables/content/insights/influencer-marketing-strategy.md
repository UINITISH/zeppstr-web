# Influencer Marketing: Strategy, Selection and the Parts Nobody Discusses

Influencer marketing works. It also has a fraud problem, a measurement problem and a compliance problem, and most published guidance addresses none of them.

This covers how to run the channel properly — including how to avoid paying for an audience that does not exist.

### Key takeaways

- Engagement quality matters more than follower count, and both can be bought.
- Smaller creators with specific audiences generally outperform large ones with broad audiences.
- Disclosure is a legal requirement in India, not a courtesy. Both the brand and the creator carry exposure.
- Attribution is genuinely difficult. Build measurement into the brief, not after the campaign.

## Why the channel works

The mechanism is borrowed trust. A recommendation from someone an audience has chosen to follow carries weight that brand advertising cannot buy directly.

It also solves a distribution problem. As organic reach on brand accounts has collapsed, creators are one of the few remaining routes to audiences without paying for every impression — and their content typically outperforms brand-produced content even when it *is* paid for.

## Choosing creators

This is where most of the outcome is determined, and where most of the money is wasted.

### Follower count is the least useful number

Reach is not the point. A creator with 20,000 people who genuinely care about a specific subject will outperform one with 500,000 casual followers for almost any commercial objective.

Tiers, roughly:

- **Nano (under 10k)** — highest engagement rates, very specific audiences, cheapest, hardest to scale.
- **Micro (10k–100k)** — usually the best return. Engaged, affordable, specific enough to target.
- **Macro (100k–1M)** — reach at higher cost, engagement typically lower.
- **Celebrity (1M+)** — awareness plays. Expensive, and rarely efficient for conversion.

For most businesses the answer is several micro-influencers rather than one macro.

### How to check whether an audience is real

Follower fraud is widespread and easy to buy. Before committing budget:

- **Engagement rate against followers.** Very low is a warning sign; suspiciously high is also a warning sign, since engagement pods inflate it.
- **Comment quality.** Generic comments and emoji strings suggest bots or pods. Real audiences write specific things.
- **Follower growth curve.** Organic growth is uneven. Sharp vertical steps usually mean purchased followers.
- **Audience geography.** Ask for the analytics screenshot. A creator selling to your Bengaluru audience whose followers are mostly overseas is not useful, whatever the number.
- **Past brand work.** Look at how those posts performed, and whether the creator works with everyone indiscriminately.

Ask for platform analytics screenshots directly. A creator unwilling to share them is telling you something.

### Fit matters more than metrics

Does this person's audience plausibly buy what you sell? Does their content style suit your brand without forcing them to write unnaturally? Have they endorsed direct competitors recently?

A mismatched partnership reads as inauthentic to the audience and damages both parties.

## Disclosure is a legal requirement

In India, influencer advertising is governed by ASCI's guidelines for influencer advertising in digital media and by the Central Consumer Protection Authority's endorsement guidelines under the Consumer Protection Act 2019.

The practical requirements:

- Any material connection — payment, free product, or any other benefit — must be disclosed.
- Disclosure must be prominent and hard to miss. Buried in a hashtag block at the end of a caption does not qualify.
- It must be in the same language as the post.
- For video, it must appear in the video itself, not only in the description.
- Endorsers must have actually used the product where they claim experience of it.

**Liability sits with the brand as well as the creator.** Contract for it explicitly, and check compliance before the post goes live rather than after.

This is not merely a legal point. Audiences generally react better to open disclosure than to a partnership they later work out was paid.

## Structuring the partnership

### Brief properly, then get out of the way

Give creators the message, the non-negotiables and the compliance requirements. Do not give them a script.

Creators know what their audience responds to. Content that has been rewritten into brand voice performs measurably worse, and the reason people pay for creator content in the first place is that it does not sound like advertising.

### Get the commercial terms in writing

- Deliverables, formats and posting dates.
- Usage rights — including whether you may run the content as paid media, and for how long. This is the term most often omitted and most often disputed later.
- Exclusivity, if any, and its duration.
- Disclosure obligations.
- Approval process and timelines.

**Usage rights deserve particular attention.** Creator content used as paid social advertising frequently outperforms brand-produced creative. If you have not licensed it for that, you cannot use it.

### Prefer relationships to one-off posts

Repeated appearances from the same creator read as genuine preference. A single sponsored post reads as an advertisement, because it is one.

## Measuring it

Attribution here is harder than in performance channels, and pretending otherwise leads to bad decisions.

**What works:**

- **Unique discount codes** per creator — clean attribution, though it captures only direct response.
- **Dedicated landing pages or links** per creator.
- **Post-purchase survey** — "where did you hear about us?" — which often reveals influence that click attribution misses entirely.
- **Branded search volume** before and after a campaign. A genuine lift in people searching your name is one of the better indicators of impact.

**What to be sceptical about:**

- Impressions and reach as success measures. They are activity.
- Engagement rate alone. It says nothing about purchase.
- Last-click attribution, which will systematically understate the channel's contribution.

Decide the measurement approach before the campaign runs. Retrofitting it produces numbers nobody trusts.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying followers rather than audiences.** Vet properly.
2. **Scripting the creator.** Destroys the reason you hired them.
3. **Ignoring disclosure.** Regulatory and reputational exposure for both parties.
4. **One-off activations.** Repetition builds credibility; single posts rarely do.
5. **Not licensing content for paid use.** Leaves the highest-value asset unusable.
6. **Measuring on reach.** Tells you nothing about whether it worked.

## Conclusion

Choose creators for audience fit rather than audience size, verify the audience is real, brief clearly and then trust them with the execution, get disclosure and usage rights right in the contract, and decide how you will measure before you start.

Done well, this is one of the more efficient channels available. Done carelessly, it is one of the easiest places to spend money on nothing at all.

## FAQ

### How do I find the right creators?

Start with people your existing customers already follow. Platform search and creator marketplaces help, but audience fit matters more than any database ranking.

### How much should I pay?

Rates vary enormously by platform, niche and deliverables. Get several quotes in your category rather than relying on published rate cards, and price against expected business outcome rather than follower count.

### Micro or macro?

Micro, for most objectives. Better engagement, better economics, more specific audiences. Macro is for awareness at scale when you have the budget for it.

### What are the disclosure rules in India?

Material connections must be disclosed prominently, in the post itself, in the same language, and in-video for video content. ASCI's influencer guidelines and the CCPA endorsement guidelines both apply, and the brand shares liability.

### How do I spot fake followers?

Check engagement rate, comment quality, growth curve and audience geography. Ask for analytics screenshots — reluctance to share them is itself informative.

### How do I measure results?

Unique codes and links for direct response, post-purchase surveys for influence that attribution misses, and branded search lift for overall impact. Decide the method before the campaign.
