# Building a Social Media Marketing Plan That Survives Contact With Reality

Most social media plans fail for a reason nobody writes about: they are not staffed for the cadence they promise. The strategy document commits to daily posting across four platforms, the person responsible has two days a month, and by week six the plan has quietly been abandoned.

A plan you can actually execute at half the ambition beats an excellent plan you cannot resource.

### Key takeaways

- Set goals tied to business outcomes, not to follower counts.
- Choose fewer platforms than you think you should.
- Budget for paid distribution — organic reach on brand pages is not a plan.
- Size the content calendar to the resource you actually have.

## Why a plan matters

Without one, social becomes reactive: posting when someone remembers, publishing whatever is to hand, measuring nothing. The account stays alive without ever compounding.

A plan makes four things explicit — what you want, who you are talking to, where, and how often — which is what allows anyone to judge later whether it worked.

## The elements

### 1. Goals tied to business outcomes

"Grow our following" is not a goal. Followers are not revenue, and on most platforms they no longer even guarantee reach.

Useful goals look like:

- Generate a stated number of qualified enquiries per month
- Reduce blended cost per acquisition by a defined amount
- Increase branded search volume by a measurable margin
- Hold a defined share of customer support conversations in-channel

Each of these can be measured, and each connects to something the business cares about.

### 2. Audience definition

Not demographics for their own sake — what they are trying to do, where they spend attention, and what would make them stop scrolling.

If you cannot describe a specific person your content is for, the content will be for nobody.

### 3. Platform selection

Choose two. Three at most.

The instinct to be everywhere produces thin presence everywhere and strength nowhere. Pick where your audience actually is and commit properly.

Note that platform choice determines resourcing more than any other decision. A video-first platform demands production capacity that a text-first platform does not.

### 4. Content strategy

Define three or four recurring content types so you are not inventing a format each time. For example: a customer or project story, a practical how-to, a behind-the-scenes look, an industry opinion.

Recurring formats are what make consistent output possible. Original concepts every time are what make plans collapse.

**Weight toward video.** Every major platform's distribution favours short vertical video, and this is not close.

### 5. Cadence you can sustain

Here is the honest arithmetic. A single properly-made short video — concept, shoot, edit, caption, publish, respond to comments — takes several hours. If one person has four days a month for social, they can produce perhaps eight strong pieces of content, not daily posts across four platforms.

Decide the cadence from the resource, not from the ambition. Then protect it.

### 6. Paid distribution

Organic reach on brand pages is minimal and will not improve. If the plan depends on free reach, it depends on something that does not exist.

Budget for paid amplification of your best-performing organic content — that content has already demonstrated it works, which makes it the safest thing to spend on.

### 7. Engagement

Someone must reply to comments and messages, within a stated time. Assign this explicitly or it will not happen.

On platforms where early engagement affects distribution, the first hour after posting matters materially.

### 8. Measurement and review

Set a monthly review against your goals from section one. Report business outcomes first, engagement second, and follower count last if at all.

## The content calendar

A calendar is a resourcing tool as much as a scheduling one.

**Build it as:**

- A recurring weekly skeleton — which content type publishes on which day
- Planned themes a month ahead
- Space left for reactive content, because it is where a good deal of the best material comes from
- Clear ownership per item

**Keep roughly two weeks of content in reserve.** The plans that survive are the ones with a buffer, because the weeks when everything goes wrong are exactly the weeks the calendar would otherwise break.

Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout Social and Later all handle scheduling adequately. The tool matters far less than having a calendar at all.

## Measuring return

### What to track

- **Traffic from social to site**, tagged with UTM parameters so it is visible in analytics
- **Enquiries or conversions attributable to social**
- **Cost per acquisition** where paid is running
- **Branded search volume** — one of the better indicators that awareness work is landing
- **Engagement quality** — saves and shares weighted well above likes, as the strongest signals of content that actually connected

### What to be careful with

Last-click attribution consistently understates social, because social usually influences a decision rather than closing it. Someone sees a post, searches your name a fortnight later, and analytics credits organic search.

Use post-purchase surveys — "how did you hear about us?" — alongside analytics. The gap between the two is often the most informative number you have.

## Engaging the audience

**Make content worth engaging with.** No amount of prompting rescues content nobody cares about.

**Ask questions you actually want answered.** Engagement bait is transparent and increasingly penalised.

**Respond properly and quickly.** Including to criticism, in public. How a brand handles a complaint publicly is watched by far more people than the complaint itself.

**Encourage user-generated content.** It is more credible than anything you publish about yourself. Nike's running community hashtags, Starbucks' partner stories and GoPro's Photo of the Day are long-running public examples of the same mechanic: give people a reason and a frame to post, then amplify what they make.

## Paid social: the essentials

1. **Target properly** — start from your own customer data. First-party custom audiences outperform interest targeting and are more resilient to privacy changes.
2. **Write copy that says something specific.**
3. **Optimise to the real objective.** Traffic objectives deliver traffic, not customers.
4. **Retarget** site visitors and engaged followers.
5. **Lead with strong visuals**, sized correctly for each placement.
6. **Test concepts, not colours.**
7. **Refresh before fatigue** — rising frequency with falling click-through is the signal.

## Staying current

Platform behaviour changes constantly, and a plan built on last year's mechanics will underperform without anyone noticing why.

Follow the platforms' own product announcements, keep a small set of credible industry sources, and — most usefully — test new formats early. Platforms consistently give disproportionate reach to features they are trying to establish, and early adopters capture it.

## Conclusion

Set goals that connect to revenue. Pick two platforms. Define recurring content formats. Size the calendar to your actual capacity. Budget for paid. Assign engagement to a person. Review monthly against the goals you set.

The plans that work are rarely the most sophisticated. They are the ones that were built for the resources the team actually has.

## FAQ

### How do I set social media goals?

Tie them to business outcomes — enquiries, cost per acquisition, branded search lift. If a goal cannot be connected to something the business cares about, it is a vanity metric.

### How many platforms should I be on?

Two, in most cases. Presence everywhere produces strength nowhere.

### How often should I post?

At the highest cadence you can maintain at quality, given real available time. Work it out from hours available, not from what competitors appear to do.

### Do I need paid?

For reliable distribution, yes. Organic reach on brand pages is minimal and will not recover.

### How do I measure ROI?

UTM tags into analytics, conversions in your CRM, plus post-purchase surveys to capture the influence that last-click attribution misses.

### What if my plan isn't working?

Check resourcing first. Most failures are execution capacity, not strategy. A plan promising more than the team can deliver fails regardless of how good the strategy was.
