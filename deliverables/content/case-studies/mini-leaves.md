# Mini Leaves — a brand and a revenue system, built together

Source of record: `Case Study by Zeppstr.pdf` and `Portfolio Zeppstr.pdf`
(Case Studies & Portfolio). Client: Mini Leaves (minileaves.com) · India ·
E-commerce, premium wooden toys.

## The situation

Mini Leaves makes premium wooden and Montessori toys for the Indian market. The
product was strong. The commercial problem was that it lived in too many places at
once — a Shopify storefront plus marketplace presences on Amazon, FirstCry and
others — with no single system holding them together.

The objective was to grow website traffic and visibility while managing a
fragmented online presence as one coherent brand rather than four disconnected
storefronts.

## The diagnosis

Premium wooden toys are a considered purchase with an educated buyer. Parents
research before they buy, and they research using category and intent language —
"Montessori wooden toys", "wooden puzzles for 3 year olds" — not brand names. A
brand nobody is searching for yet has to be discoverable through the category.

The marketplace fragmentation compounded it. Every channel was acquiring
independently, so nothing accrued. Marketplace sales built the marketplace's
customer relationship, not the brand's.

## What we did

- Implemented targeted SEO against category and intent keywords, not brand terms.
- Developed engaging, keyword-focused content mapped to how parents actually search.
- Managed the Shopify storefront alongside Amazon, FirstCry and other marketplace
  profiles as a single connected presence.
- Optimised paid campaigns to work with organic rather than duplicate it.
- Supported the brand layer with product design and social media management.

## What changed

Website traffic and visibility rose significantly, with a set of category-intent
keywords ranking in top SERP positions:

Presence across the major e-commerce platforms improved in parallel, with the

## What this proves

For a considered-purchase D2C brand, the acquisition channel and the brand layer are
not separate projects. Parents research the category, then choose a brand they
recognise from the research.

Running content, lifecycle and acquisition as one connected practice — across owned
storefront and marketplaces — is what turns scattered visibility into a system where
each channel makes the next one cheaper.
