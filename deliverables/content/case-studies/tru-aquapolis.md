# TRU Aquapolis — 75 units closed from ₹1.4 Cr of media

Eight months. ₹1.4 Cr of Meta and Google media. 6,000+ leads, 75 apartments sold at a
₹2.5 Cr average — **₹187.5 Cr in closed sales**, with ₹50–60 Cr still open in pipeline.

That ratio is 134×. It is a number most agency decks would lead with and few could
defend, so the arithmetic is set out in full below. The underlying CRM data sits under
NDA and is not reproduced here; the figures are published with the client's agreement.

**One boundary worth stating plainly:** Zeppstr ran acquisition and qualification. TRU's
own sales team closed every one of these 75 units. We do not claim the close.

## The situation

TRU Aquapolis is a premium 3 and 4 BHK lake-facing tower in East Bengaluru — the most
contested luxury corridor in India. DLF, Prestige, Sobha, Godrej, Brigade and Embassy
all bid on the same intent in the same postcodes. In a market like that, presence is the
only moat, and presence is expensive.

The brief was narrow and unsentimental: generate qualified, in-market buyer enquiries at
a cost the developer could underwrite against booking economics. Not impressions. Not
engagement. Enquiries a sales team can call.

## The diagnosis

We inherited an account that was not underperforming so much as structurally broken.

- **Zero platform-recorded conversions in February** — against 147 sitting in the CRM.
  Smart Bidding was optimising against missing data, which means it was optimising
  against nothing.
- **70% of February's ₹6.5L Google spend** went to a single brand campaign paying ₹101
  per click.
- **Every campaign was paused at handover.**

The account was not short of budget. It was short of instrumentation. Any spend increase
before fixing tracking would have compounded the waste.

## What we did

Four moves, in order. A stack, not a stunt.

**i. Build the awareness pool before chasing the lead.**
A brand-reach campaign reached 21.4 lakh Bengaluru residents at ₹0.58 per person — the
cheapest brand presence the account has ever logged.

**ii. Win the high-intent searches.**
Four disciplined Google campaigns, not forty. The UHI · 4BHK · Whitefield campaign
delivered the account's strongest unit economics at a 5.83% CTR — roughly double the
Search benchmark for luxury real estate.

**iii. Let creatives compete, then consolidate without ceremony.**
Thirty-three Meta creatives tested; two survived. By May the top two carried half the
leads in the entire account. Demand Gen was killed on data inside 21 days — a discipline
most retainer-fee agencies are not structured to recommend.

**iv. Qualify before handing over.**
Every lead was screened before it reached TRU's sales desk. Roughly 70% cleared — budget
band, configuration fit, contactability, genuine purchase intent. The remaining 30% were
never passed on. This is the step that makes the close rate below mean anything: the
sales team spent its time on buyers, not on volume.

**v. Make the brand visible everywhere.**
131 organic social pieces across two months: 106 stories, 13 posts, 12 reels.

## The arithmetic

Eight months, January to August 2026.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Media investment | ₹1.4 Cr (Meta + Google) |
| Leads generated | 6,000+ |
| Blended cost per lead | ₹2,333 |
| Qualified after screening | 70%+ (~4,200) |
| Units closed | 75 |
| Close rate on qualified leads | 1.8% |
| Average ticket | ₹2.5 Cr |
| **Closed sales value** | **₹187.5 Cr** |
| Open pipeline | ₹50–60 Cr |
| Media to closed sales | 134× |

The two middle numbers are the ones that matter. **₹2,333 per lead** and a **1.8% close
rate on qualified leads** are ordinary figures for ₹2.5 Cr property — unremarkable,
checkable, and exactly what you would expect from a well-run account.

134× is not the product of an extraordinary conversion rate. It is the product of an
ordinary conversion rate applied to a ₹2.5 Cr ticket. Any deck claiming a similar ratio
without showing the middle of the funnel is asking you to take the top line on faith.

## The forecast we published, and what actually happened

At the ninety-day mark we published an interim result: 1,690 qualified enquiries on
₹39.7L of media, and an estimated ₹34 Cr in pipeline based on a **1% close rate** — the
conservative industry floor for premium real estate.

We used the floor deliberately. Seventeen bookings was the number we were willing to
defend.

The eight-month actual was **75 units at a 1.8% close rate on qualified leads** —
comfortably ahead of the estimate we published.

We would rather be corrected upward than talk down a claim later. Most agency forecasts
move in the other direction.

## What the account looked like month to month

February was the inherited baseline: tracking broken, ₹4.6L wasted on one brand campaign
at ₹101 CPC. March reactivated Search and cut CPL 44% month on month. April pruned six
underperformers in parallel and the first real signal arrived. May was the breakout —
948 enquiries in 31 days, with the top four creatives absorbing 92% of spend. June
through August compounded against a warm audience rather than restarting cold each month.

**Channel split.** Meta delivered volume; Google delivered depth. Neither platform was
asked to do both, which is why the unit economics held as spend scaled roughly 3.5×.

**Creative concentration.** 86% of all Meta leads came from five creatives. The hero — a
single static floor-plan image — produced 440 leads in May at ₹1,378 per enquiry, 37%
below the blended average. A static image outdelivered thirty-two other ads. The skill
was not making it; it was retiring the rest.

**Organic compounding.** 32.9M views, 10.5M unique reach, +6,050 followers across April
and May. Peak engagement window 5:30–7:30 PM, posting optimised against the Bengaluru
commute scroll.

## What this proves

Three things, none of which are about the size of the number.

**Fix instrumentation before adding budget.** The account had 147 CRM leads and zero
recorded conversions. Every rupee added before that was fixed would have trained the
bidding algorithm on noise. This is the most common and most expensive failure in paid
media, and it is invisible from the dashboard.

**Qualification is where agency work should end, and it should end properly.** Passing
6,000 raw leads to a sales team would have buried them. Passing 4,200 screened ones let
them sell. The 30% we withheld is the part of the service nobody puts in a deck.

**The audience is the asset, not the quarter.** The durable output is a 31.7 lakh-strong
retargeting pool. Expected CPL against that warm audience is ₹900–₹1,500 — under half
the cost of cold acquisition. The pipeline compounds because the audience does not
restart.

That is the difference between a campaign that landed and a system that was built.
