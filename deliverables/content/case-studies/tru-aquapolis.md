# TRU Aquapolis — ₹34 Cr+ qualified pipeline on ₹39.7L of media

Source of record: `TRU_Aquapolis_Case_Study_Zeppstr.pdf` (Atelier Index I, Mar–May 2026).
All figures below are platform-verified or CRM-deposited. Nothing modelled except the
pipeline value, which is explicitly stated as a 1% close-rate floor.

## The situation

TRU Aquapolis is a premium 3 & 4 BHK lake-facing tower in East Bengaluru — the most
contested luxury corridor in India. DLF, Prestige, Sobha, Godrej, Brigade and Embassy
all bid on the same intent in the same postcodes. In a market like that, presence is
the only moat, and presence is expensive.

The brief was narrow and unsentimental: generate qualified, in-market buyer enquiries
at a cost the developer could underwrite against booking economics. Not impressions.
Not engagement. Enquiries a sales team can call.

## The diagnosis

We inherited an account that was not underperforming so much as structurally broken.

- **Zero platform-recorded conversions in February** — against 147 sitting in the CRM.
  Smart Bidding was optimising against missing data, which means it was optimising
  against nothing.
- **70% of February's ₹6.5L Google spend** went to a single brand campaign paying
  ₹101 per click.
- **Every campaign was paused at handover.**

The account was not short of budget. It was short of instrumentation. Any spend
increase before fixing tracking would have compounded the waste.

## What we did

Four moves, in order. A stack, not a stunt.

**i. Build the awareness pool before chasing the lead.**
A brand-reach campaign reached 21.4 lakh Bengaluru residents at ₹0.58 per person —
the cheapest brand presence the account has ever logged.

**ii. Win the high-intent searches.**
Four disciplined Google campaigns, not forty. The UHI · 4BHK · Whitefield campaign
delivered the account's strongest unit economics at a 5.83% CTR — roughly double the
Search benchmark for luxury real estate.

**iii. Let creatives compete, then consolidate without ceremony.**
Thirty-three Meta creatives tested; two survived. By May the top two carried half the
leads in the entire account. Demand Gen was killed on data inside 21 days — a
discipline most retainer-fee agencies are not structured to recommend.

**iv. Make the brand visible everywhere.**
131 organic social pieces across two months: 106 stories, 13 posts, 12 reels.

## What changed

Three months. Six numbers.

| Metric | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified enquiries | 1,690 (CRM-deposited) |
| Blended CPL | ₹2,348 |
| Media investment | ₹39.7L (Mar 1 – May 31, 2026) |
| Pipeline built | ₹34 Cr+ at a 1% close rate |
| Media-to-pipeline ratio | 85× |
| Retargeting pool built | 31.7L warm Bengaluru audience |

**Channel split.** Meta delivered volume: 1,248 leads at ₹2,205 CPL on ₹27.5L spend
(73.8%). Google delivered depth: 442 leads at ₹2,810 CPL on ₹12.4L spend (26.2%).
Neither platform was asked to do both — which is why the unit economics held.

**The monthly arc.** February was the inherited baseline: tracking broken, ₹4.6L wasted
on one brand campaign at ₹101 CPC. March reactivated Search and cut CPL 44% month on
month. April pruned six underperformers in parallel and the first real signal arrived.
May was the breakout — 948 enquiries in 31 days, the account's best month on record,
with the top four creatives absorbing 92% of spend.

**Creative concentration.** 86% of all Meta leads came from five creatives. The hero —
a single static floor-plan image — produced 440 leads in May at ₹1,378 per enquiry,
37% below the three-month blended average. A static image outdelivered thirty-two
other ads. The skill was not making it; it was retiring the rest.

**Organic compounding.** 32.9M views, 10.5M unique reach, +6,050 followers across April
and May (+47% MoM growth). Peak engagement window: 5:30–7:30 PM, posting optimised
against the Bengaluru commute scroll.

## What this proves

Reach across two platforms hit 1.44 Cr impressions and 1,26,056 clicks, which converted
to 1,690 qualified enquiries and — at the conservative 1% industry close rate for
premium real estate — approximately 17 bookings and ₹34 Cr+ in pipeline.

For every ₹1 of media, roughly ₹85 in qualified pipeline value.

But the durable asset is not the quarter's leads. It is the 31.7 lakh-strong
retargeting pool. Next quarter's expected CPL against that warm audience is
₹900–₹1,500 — less than half the cost of cold acquisition. The pipeline compounds
because the audience does not restart.

That is the difference between a campaign that landed and a system that was built.
