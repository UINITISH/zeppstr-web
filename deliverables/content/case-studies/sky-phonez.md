# Sky Phonez — a storefront built for SKU depth

Source of record: `Portfolio Zeppstr.pdf` (Case Studies & Portfolio), Web
Development section. Client: Sky Phonez · Australia · Consumer technology
e-commerce.

## The situation

Sky Phonez sells consumer technology in the Australian market across a wide and
deep product range. The commercial problem in that category is not persuasion — a
buyer arriving to compare a specific handset already intends to buy something. The
problem is whether they can find the exact variant they want and complete the
purchase without friction.

They needed a storefront built for that, from the ground up.

## The diagnosis

Extensive SKU counts break most e-commerce templates in a predictable way.

Navigation designed for forty products becomes unusable at four hundred. Variant
handling that works for two colours falls apart across model, storage, condition
and carrier. Search that returns approximate matches is worse than no search when
the buyer knows precisely what they want. And every one of those failures happens
after the click has been paid for — so the cost lands on acquisition while the
symptom looks like a conversion problem.

The requirement was a system that stays coherent as the catalogue grows, rather
than a design that photographs well with a curated dozen products in it.

## What we did

Custom-built the site end to end, rather than adapting a template:

- **Custom UI/UX design** — a tailored interface built for engaging, intuitive
  browsing, designed around how a technology buyer actually narrows a decision.
- **E-commerce integration** — a fully integrated store supporting smooth product
  browsing and purchasing, with the checkout treated as part of the experience
  rather than a bolt-on.
- **Extensive SKU listings** — efficient management and display across a wide
  product range, so catalogue depth is an asset to the shopper instead of an
  obstacle.

## What changed

A complete custom storefront delivered for the Australian market: bespoke
interface, integrated commerce, and a product architecture that handles extensive
SKU listings without degrading as the range expands.

## What this proves

In high-SKU retail, the storefront is not a brochure — it is the conversion
mechanism. Every unnecessary step between a buyer who already knows what they want
and the checkout is paid for twice: once in media cost to bring them, and again in
the margin of the sale that never happens.

Building for catalogue depth from the first line of code is cheaper than
retrofitting it after the range has grown and the acquisition cost has already
been spent.
