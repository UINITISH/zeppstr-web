# Prohance — selling measurement to people who fear being measured

Client: Prohance · India · Workforce analytics and operations enablement.
Public positioning, compliance posture and named customers below are taken from
the client's own website and are independently verifiable.

## The situation

Prohance sells a New-Age Workforce Analytics and Operations Enablement Platform —
real-time productivity insights, AI adoption and ROI visibility, data-driven
decision intelligence. It carries serious enterprise proof: over 450,000 users
across 55 countries, and named customers including Firstsource, GeBBS, JLL,
Sutherland and eiDesign.

The platform is credible. The category it sells into is one of the most
rhetorically hostile in enterprise software.

## The diagnosis

Workforce analytics has a positioning problem that no feature list solves.

The buyer — an operations or transformation leader — wants visibility into how
work actually happens. The end users are the employees being measured, and the
public conversation about this category is dominated by the word "surveillance."
Every vendor in the space is one bad headline from being recast as spyware.

That shapes the entire marketing problem in three ways.

**Trust has to be structural, not asserted.** Prohance leads with "Privacy First
— Your Data. Your Control," backed by GDPR, SOC, ISO 27001 and HIPAA compliance.
That is the correct instinct: in this category, compliance badges are not
procurement hygiene, they are the core message.

**The buyer needs external cover.** An operations leader championing a
productivity platform internally is taking reputational risk. They need
third-party validation — industry whitepapers, global productivity benchmarks,
named peers already using it — to make the case to their own stakeholders. That
is a content and authority problem, not an ads problem.

**Enterprise logos do the heaviest lifting.** JLL and Sutherland on the homepage
answer the unspoken question — *is this safe to buy?* — faster than any feature
comparison. The marketing job is to get those logos in front of the right buyer
early and often.

## What this proves

In categories where the product is politically sensitive inside the buyer's own
organisation, the constraint on growth is rarely awareness or lead volume. It is
whether the champion has enough external cover to win the internal argument.

That reframes the marketing brief entirely. Benchmarks, whitepapers, compliance
proof and peer logos are not brand assets in this category — they are sales
enablement, and they belong at the top of the funnel rather than the bottom.
