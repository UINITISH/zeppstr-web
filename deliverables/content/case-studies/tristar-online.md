# Tristar Online — competing on category breadth against Amazon

Client: Tristar Online · Australia · Multi-category consumer e-commerce.
Public positioning below is taken from the client's own storefront and is
independently verifiable.

## The situation

Tristar Online is an Australian multi-category retailer: mobiles, watches,
appliances, games and accessories, with Apple iPhones featured on the homepage.
It runs free shipping on all Australian orders, an "Our Stores" marketplace bar
including Amazon, and a conventional category-plus-search navigation.

Its own storefront tells you the strategic position honestly. It sells the same
products, in the same country, as the largest retailer on earth — and also sells
through that retailer.

## The diagnosis

**Zero product differentiation, by definition.** An iPhone from Tristar is
identical to an iPhone from anywhere. When the product is fixed, competition
collapses onto price, availability, delivery and trust. Three of those four are
structurally hard for an independent to win against Amazon.

Which means the winnable ground is narrow and specific: the long-tail SKU Amazon
deprioritises, the variant that is out of stock elsewhere, the buyer who wants a
local retailer, and the searches where a specialist page beats a generic listing.

**Marketplace presence cuts both ways.** Selling on Amazon buys reach and costs
margin — and, more importantly, costs the customer relationship. Every marketplace
sale builds Amazon's data asset, not Tristar's. The strategic question is not
whether to be there, but which SKUs justify renting that audience versus which
should be driven to the owned storefront where the margin and the repeat
relationship live.

**Multi-category breaks focused SEO.** Mobiles, watches, appliances and games have
different buyers, different seasonality, different competitors and different
content requirements. Spreading effort evenly across all four is how a mid-size
retailer ends up ranking for nothing. The discipline is choosing which category
to actually own.

**Free shipping is a margin decision disguised as a marketing one.** In thin-margin
consumer electronics, an unconditional free-shipping promise on every order sets a
floor under basket size that the merchandising has to earn back.

## What this proves

An independent retailer cannot beat Amazon at breadth, price or logistics, and
strategies built on trying are expensive ways to lose slowly.

What is winnable is the long tail, the owned relationship, and the categories
where a specialist page genuinely serves the buyer better than a marketplace
listing. That requires deciding what *not* to compete on — which is the hardest
instruction to give a retailer whose instinct is to stock more.
